# Bill Sample Selection: LLM Approach

## Overview

This document describes the bill selection pipeline, starting from the full bill universe, and how an LLM-based classifier replaces the original keyword-matching approach.

---

## Stage 1: Pre-filter (unchanged)

**Input:** 270,115 bills from the 93rd–116th Congress (1973–2019), stored in local SQLite databases (`data/bill_subjects.db`, `data/bill.db`, `data/bill_text.db`).

A bill passes the pre-filter if it satisfies **both** conditions:

**Condition 1 — Policy area** must be one of:
- `finance and financial sector`
- `law`
- `crime and law enforcement`

**Condition 2 — Subjects** must contain at least one of ~35 tags including: `securities fraud`, `corporate governance`, `financial disclosure`, `insider trading`, `bank fraud`, `accounting and auditing`, `corporations`, `securities and exchange commission`, etc. (full list in `selected_bill_policies_subjects.json`).

These are Congress.gov metadata tags assigned to each bill, not text matches.

**Output:** 3,932 bills pass the pre-filter. This is further reduced to **2,726 bills** after requiring that each bill has both a summary text and a full text available in the database (bills from congresses 93–102 are largely excluded as govinfo.gov only has full text from congress 103 onward).

The 2,726 bills are the input to Stage 2.

---

## Stage 2: Bill Selection

### Original approach — Keyword matching

The original script (`3_select_export_bills.py`) searches for keywords within the bill text. A bill is included if any keyword appears in either the **summary text** (checked first) or the **full text** (checked as fallback).

Keywords (`keywords.txt`):
```
securities fraud
accounting fraud
accounting statement
financial statement
corporate regulation
financial regulation
financial disclosure
corporate disclosure
insider trading
corporate
securities law
corporate fraud
securities and exchange commission
```

**Output:** 816 bills selected out of 2,726.

**Limitation:** Exact string matching misses bills that are substantively about financial regulation but use different terminology (e.g., "Sarbanes-Oxley", "short selling restrictions"), and may include false positives where a keyword appears incidentally.

---

### New approach — LLM classification

**Motivation:** Replace exact keyword matching with semantic reasoning to reduce both false negatives (missed relevant bills) and false positives (incidentally matched bills).

**Input data** (generated by `prepare_llm_input.py`):
- `llm_input_metadata.csv` — 2,726 rows × 7 columns: `bill_id`, `congress_term`, `introduced_at`, `policy_area`, `subjects`, `official_title`, `short_title`
- `llm_input_texts/` — 5,452 `.txt` files, two per bill: `{bill_id}_summary.txt` and `{bill_id}_full_text.txt`

**Prompt (Option B — independent judge):**

```
You are a legal researcher studying congressional bills related to the regulation of
corporations and financial markets.

A bill is relevant to your study if it primarily concerns:
1. How corporations must disclose financial information to investors or regulators
2. Rules governing securities markets (trading, fraud, manipulation)
3. Oversight powers of financial regulators (SEC, CFTC, FDIC, etc.)
4. Accounting standards or auditing requirements for public companies
5. Corporate governance rules (board duties, executive compensation, shareholder rights)

A bill is NOT relevant if it only incidentally mentions a corporation or financial
institution as one actor among many (e.g., a general criminal fraud bill, a tax
reform bill).

Bill title: {official_title}
Bill summary: {summary_text}
Bill full text: {full_text}

Is this bill relevant? Answer YES or NO, then explain in one sentence.
```

**Output:** *(to be completed after LLM run)*
