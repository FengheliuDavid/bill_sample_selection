
You are a legislative analyst. You are interested in understanding how bills proposed in the U.S. Congress topically align with issues related to enforcement on a variety of topics.
 
Identify whether the U.S. Congressional bill below aligns with any of the Violation Tracker categories and, if so which ones. These categories reflect types of **corporate misconduct** targeted by government enforcement actions (DOJ, SEC, EPA, OSHA, FTC, etc.) against companies and employers — whether privately-held or publicly-traded. The key distinction is non-governmental: government agencies, military branches, and public-sector entities are excluded, but large publicly-traded corporations (e.g., ExxonMobil, JPMorgan, Boeing) are squarely within scope.
 
A bill qualifies if it is substantively related to the type of corporate conduct associated with the underlying Violation Tracker violation — whether by directly regulating or enforcing that conduct, or by introducing tax incentives that encourage or discourage it. For example, a wage and hour violation that relates to overtime issues, underpayment of wages, etc. might be related to a bill that relates to worker pay-related issues (overtime protections and exemptions, restrictions on things like "clopening" schedules, tax credits for employers who voluntarily expand benefits, etc. -- this is not exhaustive but just an example).

## Categories

1. Wage Hour & Benefits — ONLY applies when a non-governmental employer is the direct subject accused of: failing to pay required wages, overtime, or tips; failing to give employees adequate advance notice before mass layoffs or plant closings; mismanaging or looting employee pension, 401k, or retirement benefit plans; or retaliating against employees for taking protected family, medical, or parental leave. Acts covered include but are not limited to FLSA (minimum wage, overtime), WARN Act (layoff notice), ERISA (pension/retirement plan obligations), and FMLA (leave retaliation). Bills primarily addressing government employee compensation, military pay, veterans' healthcare or education programs, or any benefits administered by the VA, DoD, or other government agencies to service members or veterans do not involve employer misconduct and do not qualify.

   The following bill types do NOT qualify, even if they mention "wages," "benefits," "employees," or "pensions":
   - VA or DoD appropriations bills funding veterans' hospitals, nursing homes, or healthcare programs — these fund government services, not regulate private employer conduct.
   - Bills adjusting Medicare or Medicaid payment rates to hospitals or providers — these set government reimbursement rates, not employer wage obligations.
   - Bills offering employers a tax credit for providing benefits unrelated to wage/hour obligations (e.g., general business investment credits, adoption tax benefits, AMT adjustments) — these do not relate to private employer wage or benefit conduct and do not qualify.
   - Bills addressing federal or state government employee pay, civil service benefits, or public-sector pension systems — the government is not a "non-governmental employer" under this category.
   - Bills financing government trust funds for miner benefits or similar programs (e.g., Black Lung Disability Trust Fund) — these address fund solvency, not employer wage violations.
   - Bills about Social Security, TANF, or other public assistance programs — these are government benefit programs, not private employer obligations.

2. Employment Discrimination — Legislation addressing employer discrimination against job applicants or employees based on race, gender, age, national origin, disability, or other protected characteristics; or employer-side FCRA violations during hiring (e.g., failure to notify applicants before obtaining background reports).

3. Labor Relations — Legislation addressing NLRB unfair labor practice violations of workers' collective bargaining and organizing rights; employer retaliation against whistleblowers who report safety or fraud violations; or misuse of H-1B and other visa programs to displace U.S. workers.

4. Workplace Safety — Legislation addressing OSHA and MSHA enforcement for hazardous working conditions across general industry and mining, including toxic exposures, pandemic-related safety failures, and violations of federal mine safety standards.

5. Consumer Safety — Legislation addressing FDA, CPSC, and related enforcement for defective or dangerous drugs, medical devices, consumer products, and food; failure to report defects or issue recalls; contaminated food shipments; or tobacco marketing violations.

6. Transportation Safety — Legislation addressing FRA, FAA, and NHTSA enforcement for safety violations in railroad operations, aviation maintenance, and motor vehicle manufacturing; or DOJ enforcement against private prisoner transport companies.

7. Infrastructure & Nuclear Safety — Legislation addressing NRC and DOE enforcement for nuclear facility safety violations; PHMSA and state PUC enforcement for gas pipeline safety failures; or FERC enforcement for dam safety and hydroelectric license violations.

8. Campus Safety — Legislation addressing Department of Education enforcement against universities specifically for Clery Act failures, including inadequate disclosure of campus crimes or mishandling of sexual assault cases (e.g., Michigan State/Nassar, Penn State/Sandusky). Does NOT include general K-12 or higher education policy, curriculum standards, teacher recruitment, or school funding.

9. Banking AML & Sanctions — Legislation addressing federal and state banking regulator enforcement for unsafe or deceptive banking practices, anti-money-laundering compliance failures, criminal money laundering, or OFAC sanctions violations involving prohibited countries or designated persons.

10. Securities & Investor Protection — Legislation addressing SEC, CFTC, and FINRA enforcement for securities fraud, insider trading, failures to protect investor assets, and improper offerings; including toxic securities (RMBS, CDOs) and unregistered crypto offerings. Does NOT include intelligence appropriations, defense authorization bills, military procurement legislation, surveillance reform legislation, or government national security authorities — even if they contain financial oversight, contractor accountability, or business record provisions.

11. Accounting Financial Reporting & Tax — Legislation addressing SEC enforcement for falsifying financial statements; DOJ Trustee enforcement for fraudulent bankruptcy claims; or state and local tax compliance failures including underpayments and business registration violations.

12. Fraud, Corruption & Criminal Conduct — Legislation addressing FCPA foreign bribery, domestic kickbacks, general fraud (overbilling, contract fraud), racketeering, false statements, obstruction of justice, civil contempt of court orders, theft, postal violations, or defamation.

13. Intellectual Property & Trade Secrets — Legislation addressing theft of proprietary business information (often involving foreign actors), copyright infringement, counterfeit goods trafficking, or DMCA violations.

14. Consumer Protection — Legislation addressing state AG and FTC enforcement for deceptive marketing, predatory lending (payday, mortgage), housing code failures, gambling and cannabis licensing violations, insurance non-compliance, privacy breaches, disability access failures, telecommunications and utility service violations, or credit reporting accuracy failures.

15. Competition-related Offenses — Legislation addressing antitrust enforcement for price-fixing, bid-rigging, and predatory pricing; FERC/CFTC enforcement for energy market manipulation; customs and trade enforcement against companies caught evading duties or violating Jones Act requirements; or USDA enforcement against companies for agricultural marketing standard violations. Does NOT include routine Congressional tariff legislation — any bill whose title contains phrases like "suspend temporarily the duty on", "reduce the duty on", "extend the suspension of duty on", or "amend the Harmonized Tariff Schedule" is categorically excluded, as these are routine policy adjustments rather than enforcement actions against a company. Does NOT include general agriculture subsidy, commodity payment, farm program, or defense authorization bills.

16. Air Violation — Legislation addressing EPA Clean Air Act enforcement for air emissions violations and permit non-compliance at industrial facilities; NHTSA fines against automakers for failing to meet Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) standards; or EPA enforcement for improper handling or disposal of asbestos at demolition and construction sites.

17. Water Violation — Legislation addressing EPA Clean Water Act enforcement for discharging pollutants (including coal ash and industrial wastewater) into rivers, lakes, and waterways; Safe Drinking Water Act enforcement at public water systems; enforcement and litigation for PFAS, PCB, and MTBE groundwater contamination; EPA enforcement against large livestock operations (CAFOs) for agricultural runoff; or enforcement for oil spills from vessels and pipelines.

18. Earth Violation — Legislation addressing EPA RCRA enforcement for improper storage, handling, or disposal of hazardous and radioactive waste; underground petroleum storage tank violations; DOT/PHMSA enforcement for pipeline safety failures; BSEE enforcement for offshore oil and gas drilling violations; EPA and state enforcement for environmental damage from mining operations; EPA FIFRA enforcement for unlicensed or mislabeled pesticide distribution; state enforcement for solid waste management failures; or federal enforcement for disturbing archaeological resources on public or tribal lands.

19. Lead Violation — Legislation addressing enforcement and litigation for products containing excessive lead levels, including children's jewelry, school supplies, and lead-based paint.

20. Energy Efficiency — Legislation addressing DOE enforcement for failures to comply with mandatory energy efficiency standards for appliances and equipment.

21. Animal Welfare — Legislation addressing USDA and DOJ enforcement for violations of the Animal Welfare Act at research facilities, breeders, and animal dealers.

22. Government Contracting — Legislation addressing False Claims Act enforcement against contractors and healthcare providers for fraudulent billing of federal programs, or DOI enforcement against oil and gas companies for underreporting production and underpaying federal land lease royalties.

23. Healthcare Fraud & Compliance — Legislation addressing DOJ, FDA, HHS OIG, CMS, and DEA enforcement for pharmaceutical off-label marketing fraud, Medicare and Medicaid billing violations, nursing home care failures, controlled substances tracking failures, healthcare price transparency violations, or fetal tissue trafficking.

24. Government Reporting & Compliance — Legislation addressing FEC and state enforcement for illegal corporate campaign contributions and failure to disclose political advertising; or agency enforcement for failure to file required regulatory reports such as CFIUS foreign ownership disclosures and lobbying contribution reports. Covers ONLY these narrow FEC/CFIUS/lobbying disclosure contexts. Does NOT include any other type of reporting — not agency or departmental reports to Congress, not environmental permitting reports, not government administrative procedures, not military affairs, defense authorization bills, military appropriations, intelligence agency budgets, FISA or surveillance reform legislation, or government administrative restructuring.

25. National Security & Cybercrime — Legislation addressing DOJ and State Department enforcement for corporate payments to designated terrorist organizations, unauthorized transfer of classified military technology to foreign countries, or unauthorized intrusion into competitor or third-party computer systems.

26. Exploitation & Trafficking — Legislation addressing civil and criminal enforcement against companies that facilitated or financially benefited from sex trafficking or sexual exploitation of minors.

27. Firearms Violation — Legislation addressing civil litigation against firearms manufacturers for irresponsible marketing that facilitates violence, or enforcement against unlicensed ghost gun manufacturers.

## Output

Return ONLY a JSON object with three fields: `categories`, `bill_type`, and `reason`.

**categories** — a list of matching category numbers (1–27). If multiple apply, include all. If none apply, return an empty list.

**bill_type** — classify how the bill primarily relates to the matched categories. Use one of:
- `"enforcement"` — the bill affects the stringency of laws or regulations governing the relevant corporate misconduct (e.g., raising the minimum wage, expanding overtime protections, strengthening ERISA fiduciary duties, tightening WARN Act notice requirements).
- `"tax_credit_introduction"` — the main way the bill connects to a matched category is by introducing a new tax credit that incentivizes employers toward better behavior (e.g., a new credit for voluntarily offering part-time employees healthcare benefits, a credit for employers who retain workers called up for military reserve duty). The Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC), which incentivizes hiring from disadvantaged groups, is an example of a tax_credit_introduction bill in the Employment Discrimination category.
- `"tax_credit_removal"` — the bill removes or reduces an existing tax credit of the above type.
- `""` (empty string) — if categories is empty (no category matched), bill_type must also be empty. If a bill touches on a related topic but does not clearly fit enforcement or tax credit, treat it as not matching any category and return an empty list.

**reason** — one sentence explaining why the bill was assigned to those categories and that bill_type. If categories is empty, briefly state why it does not qualify.

Examples:

{"categories": [1], "bill_type": "tax_credit_introduction", "reason": "Introduces a new tax credit incentivizing private employers to voluntarily offer paid family leave, relating to FMLA-adjacent employer conduct."}

No category fits:
{"categories": [], "bill_type": "", "reason": "Bill appropriates funds for VA hospital operations; no private employer wage or benefit obligation is involved."}
